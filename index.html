The Greatest Estate Developer

Has no right being thing good, also, made by users BK_Moon, Manggi, and serialized by Naver Series

Read More: The Greatest Estate Developer

The Greatest Estate Developer follows Kim Suho, a civil engineering student who wakes up inside the body of Lloyd Frontera, a lazy, widely hated noble whose family is drowning in massive debt. Using his real-world engineering knowledge and a mysterious system interface that rewards his building projects, Lloyd decides to design infrastructure, construct buildings, and save the region from bankruptcy to secure a life of easy luxury. In other words, he singlehandedly blasts society 700 years forward because he wants to be lazy. He journeys through the bottoms of the ocean, goes to hell and back, and somehow manages to glitch out the world so bad they make an anti-Lloyd plot armor device, and is followed every step of the way by his straight faced knight Javier and his own little pet friends. Unfortunately, Javier becomes the same species of maniac Lloyd is all too fast. Frankly, though, the end of this book just made me sit in my bed for a good few hours and think.

The Boy on the Wooden Box

How the Impossible Became Possible on Schindler's List, a memoir by Leon Leyson

Read More: The Boy on the Wooden Box

The Boy on the Wooden Box is a memoir by Leon Leyson/Oscar Schindler, who details his survival as one of the youngest individuals on Oskar Schindler’s list(A list of people saved by Oscar Schindler) during the Holocaust. Leon Leyson had to face many hardships, including watching his eldest brother leave to never return again, helplessly watching his favorite brother walk to his own death willingly, and being starved near-to-death. After enduring the horror circuses known as Kraków Ghetto and Płaszów concentration camp following the 1939 Nazi invasion, Leyson and his family were saved by factory owner Oscar Schindler, who employed them and protected nearly 1,200 other Jews from certain death. When the book took place, Leon was so small that to operate a factory machine he had to stand on a wooden box, hence the name. He believes he is extremely good at handling the machines. He’s happy. He gets to eat and live without worry of being killed. He’s safe, or at least he thought, until a cold, extremely unspecified, and fateful day of October 1944.

How To: Absurd Scientific Advice For Common Real-World Problems

by Randall Munroe

Read More: How To: Absurd Scientific Advice For Common Real-World Problems

This nonfiction book uses real, advanced physics and data science to solve everyday problems in the most ridiculous ways possible. The author takes normal tasks, like moving to a new house, making friends, or landing an airplane, and calculates how to do them using extreme methods like laser beams, rocket boosters, or geological forces. By using actual scientific equations to answer completely absurd questions, the book serves as a hilarious satire of traditional "how-to" manuals while teaching genuine science principles along the way. A personal favorite of mine among all of the nonfiction I have read, which means its the best of maybe seven or eight books.

The Magesterium: The Iron Trial

by Holly Black and Cassandra Clare

Read More: The Magesterium

The Magisterium is a book series about a boy called Callum Hunt who finds out he is able to wield a mysterious power known as “Magic”. All people who can ever use magic, including Callum’s very own father, must go to a school called the Magisterium to learn how to control it if they want to keep their magic. But, Callum does not want to go there, so he tries to fail on purpose… but enters in first class. Eventually, he finds out his soul hosts an… odd, to say the least, secret of the world. A secret of himself. A secret of the Man Who Defied Death, the soul swapper and chaos mage, Constantine Madden. This book honestly changed my viewpoint a lot, due to myself overthinking one singular line. “Everything has Chaos in it. Not all chaos is inherently bad, in fact, life would not exist without chaos. Chaos is simply a variable, one that you can control, Callum,” This would be a spoiler… but the pacing for this series is absolutely ATROCIOUS, it took THREE books for it to be confirmed Constantine Madden IS Callum Hunt, yet it got teased in the first 10 chapters, TWICE. And it IMMEDIATELY tells us as readers that Callum is one of the few people who can control chaos, and that he’s a prodigy genius teenager, blah blah blah. Personally, the only thing that I like about this book is how it views chaos as neutral instead of inherently bad. Nothing else is of interest to me in this book. Nothing else is really ACTUALLY built upon.

Orbiting Jupiter

by Gary D. Schmidt

Read More: Orbiting Jupiter

Orbiting Jupiter is a masterclass story by the paramount of an author known as Gary D. Schmidt. It is narrated by a 12-year-old boy named Jack, whose family takes in a 14-year-old foster brother named Joseph. Joseph has had an incredibly rough life: he just came out of a juvenile detention facility, he has a history of trauma, and he is already a father to a baby girl named Jupiter, whom he has never been allowed to meet. While the town, the school, and the teachers immediately judge Joseph and treat him like a dangerous criminal, Jack and his parents choose to see him for who he actually is. On their rural farm, Joseph slowly learns to trust again, bonds with their farm animals, and forms an incredibly fierce, loyal brotherhood with Jack. The core of the book is Joseph's desperate, unwavering mission to find his daughter Jupiter. It is a incredibly moving, fast-paced, but devastating story about unconditional love, the flaws of the foster system, and how cruel the world can be to kids who just need someone to believe in them. It is pretty short, but the ending packs a massive emotional punch that will honestly leave you sitting there staring at the wall for a while. In my opinion, one of the best books I've ever read. The imagery is so strong that you can easily imagine everything in the story as if you were in it.
